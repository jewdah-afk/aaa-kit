How the points are made

  1. Three flat views. ortho_map.py cuts the silhouette out of the front, side and top renders (the brick pipeline makes them as ortho_front, ortho_side, ortho_top).
  2. One grid. The views are brought to one scale through the axes they share, then a cell is kept where front, side and top all cover it. The front and top masks are made symmetric first.
  3. Numbered vertices. Every corner of an exposed cell face gets an ID. Its pixel is exact: front u = L + a·x, v = T + a·(Y − y), side u = L + a·z, top v = T + a·(Z − z). The cyan dots are those vertices.
  4. Colour. Each surface cell takes the pixel of the flat view that faces it and sees it first (front, back, side or top), snapped to 20 colours.
  5. Reveal. Cells build in rising F = 80·Y2 + 16·Z2 + (X2 − gridWidth)² on doubled cell centres: a curved front that climbs from the feet and closes in from the wing tips.
  6. Limits. Three silhouettes cannot show hollows (the open mouth, the gap under a wing), so those stay filled, and glow halos in a render count as model. Replace the grid with the real mesh when one exists.
  1. Cut out the silhouettes. hull_ref.py removes the plain background from the front picture and the 3/4 picture.
  2. Find the cameras. The front camera is fixed; the 3/4 camera's angles and position are searched until the carved shape fills both silhouettes.
  3. Carve with symmetry. A voxel stays only if it and its mirror twin land inside both silhouettes. Mirroring makes two pictures work like four views.
  4. Trim what no picture shows. Voxels whose colour disagrees between the two pictures are peeled off, and only what lies within 12 voxels behind a seen surface is kept, so the wings stay sheets instead of blocks.
  5. Colour from the pixels. Each surface voxel takes the pixel of the picture that sees it most face-on, then colours snap to 20. Parts no picture shows get the model's common dark tone.
  6. Outline vertices. The front silhouette is traced, reduced to corner points, mirrored, and each point is lifted onto the carved surface.
  7. Transform. Source bricks and slots are each sorted along a Morton curve and paired in that order, so no slot is used twice. Slots start bottom-up; at the end every brick sits exactly on its slot.
  8. Limits. Two pictures cannot show the back or the tail, so the back is a dark shell that follows the front and the tail is missing. The camera angles are taken from the render pipeline (35 and 14 degrees), because two pictures alone do not pin them down. A side or back picture would fix both.
  1. Run the model's maths, skip the renderer. blueprint.py executes the brick script's shape code (blended ellipsoids, capsule chains, wing triangles) with numpy only.
  2. Joints. The rig's bone ends become named vertices; the bones are the lines between them.
  3. Outline. Surface bricks on every 12th Z plane and Y plane, plus the centre profile, from the +x half, then reflected across x = 0.
  4. Slots. The grid is reduced to one voxel per 4 bricks, only the shell is kept, and each slot takes its most common surface colour and a baked top light.
  5. Transform. Source bricks and slots are each sorted along a Morton curve and paired in that order, so neighbours stay neighbours and no slot is used twice. Slots start bottom-up; at the end every brick sits exactly on its slot.
  6. Proof. The two picture views use the pipeline's own camera formula, so the same 3D points land on both renders of the model.
